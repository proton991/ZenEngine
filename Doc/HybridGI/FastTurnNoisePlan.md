# Eliminating visible flashes during fast camera turns

**Research and implementation plan · 25 November 2026 · Baseline `947f972b` · Proposed**

Start with the lamp-lit ledge in New Sponza. It is a repeatable, measured error in diffuse bounce and survives resets of the probe’s lighting history. Next address thin surfaces that intermittently lose their diffuse lighting. Diagnose the lantern separately in the raster and composition passes. Each change gets its own capture and decision before the next change starts.

For this milestone, **eliminated** means zero reproducible GI flashes above the existing detector threshold on the specified camera paths, whole-image sparkle counts within the settled range for each pose, and no visible streak, shimmer or loss of lighting in normal-speed playback. This is a testable claim about the qualified scenes and settings. Finite ray sampling leaves residual variance, and a detector alone cannot establish perceptual equivalence across arbitrary games.

## What the current renderer does

| Native 4K New Sponza, Optimal, RTX 5080 | Measured on the current shaders |
|---|---|
| Six-frame 180° yaw | 24 bright clusters at 90°, 31 at 120°, 33 covering 53 pixels on arrival, 16 at +4 frames, 2 at +64. Instant 180° cut had 8. |
| Settled reverse pose | Five held-out frames produced 2, 0, 0, 0 and 0 clusters. RGB RMS against the other four frames was 0.065–0.083 levels on the 0–255 display scale. This small sample establishes an initial floor. |
| Ordinary panning | At repeated poses, cycle-to-cycle RGB RMS was 0.706. In 0.518% of pixels at least one channel changed by more than four levels. |
| Settled accuracy | Mean total-light bias was −0.85%, −0.80% and −6.85% at center, 20° and 180°. Diffuse GI bias was −4.37%, −4.65% and −7.97%. |
| Frame time | Warmed native 4K pan 46.60 FPS median over three launches. GPU median 21.59 ms, p95 21.92 ms over 80 frames. The 60 FPS p95 target needs another 5.25 ms. |

The turn and settled-noise tests used normal maps, HDR lighting, an 8-pixel probe grid, 162,000 probes and a 4,194,304-cell world cache. The path-traced comparison uses matched vertex normals. Captures with a 1,048,576-cell cache form a separate comparison. [Raw turn measurements](../../build/turn-noise-20261125/review-current/review-metrics.json) · [Settled floor](../../build/turn-noise-20261125/review-current/settled-noise-floor.json) · [Production measurements and path-traced references](../../build/gi-production-check-20261125-1755/assessment.md)

Three observations determine the order of work.

1. **Lamp-lit bounce.** On a narrow ledge, a newly placed probe reads 65–80 on the bounce debug view against a settled value near 30. The overshoot lasts about ten frames and can take about 60 to decay. Resetting history, disabling spatial filtering and bypassing cache feedback each preserve the overshoot. Disabling the light-guided directions removes that streak but raises the full-turn recovery count to 168 from roughly 60–80 in that experiment. A per-ray clamp increased darkened pixels on arrival from 0.39% to 1.61%. Keep the light contribution and measure which samples produce the tail. [Ablations, follow-up items 1–2](../../build/turn-noise-20261125/results.md)
2. **Thin-surface coverage.** About 0.14% of settled reverse-view pixels in the coverage diagnostic receive no diffuse GI because there is no compatible probe. A changing probe footprint briefly lights some of them. A relaxed depth/normal fallback reduced uncovered pixels from 0.144% to 0.106% but left the observed flashes. A dark settled pixel can itself be wrong. [Coverage diagnostic](../../build/turn-noise-20261125/zero-gi-1600.png) · [Fallback experiment](../../build/turn-noise-20261125/results.md)
3. **Lantern.** Three pixels flash with the probe GI terms removed and GPU culling disabled. Their remaining producer is unknown. Track them in the whole-frame count through final acceptance. [Lantern investigation, follow-up item 5](../../build/turn-noise-20261125/results.md)

## Implementation sequence

### 0. Establish a repeatable failure and acceptance suite

Extend the existing fast-motion harness and component scorer. Bring the settled-pose reference and centered 9×9-ring scoring from the saved investigation into the tracked tools. The existing fast-motion harness defaults to its own HDR setup, async settings and a subset of angles; add an explicit production replay that preserves the benchmark setup and captures every sweep frame. Check parity by rescoring the saved PPMs and reproducing 33 clusters and 53 pixels at landing before changing shaders. [Motion harness](../../tools/native_gi_fast_motion.py) · [Component scorer](../../tools/gi_spark_metric.py) · [Exact scoring used for these numbers](../../build/turn-noise-20261125/score.py)

- Replay the fixed-eye turn after 1,024 warm-up frames. Capture 30°, 60°, 90°, 120°, 150°, 180°, an instant cut, and every recovery frame through +16, then +32 and +64. Run both directions, first visit and return. Add isolated 90° and 120° landings so moving and just-stopped noise are distinguishable. Keep one mirrored trajectory and one different lamp/ledge arrangement for final qualification.
- For each pose and build, use five settled frames for the reference and a disjoint set for the noise floor. At final qualification use at least 30 held-out settled samples per pose across five launches and vary the sampling phase independently of scene state. Repeated launches alone can repeat the same anchor seed. Match camera matrices, exposure, scene time and light/geometry state. Inspect a reference-rendered crop of the ledge, seam and lantern whenever the settled image is itself questionable.
- Count 8-connected bright clusters using channel excess above 24 levels and contrast above 16 relative to the 9×9 ring excluding its 3×3 center. Also record cluster size, peak, screen position and lifetime, dark deviations below reference by more than four levels, RGB RMS, and linear sky/diffuse-bounce/specular terms. Track the same feature across frames using receiver IDs and reprojection. Keep raw counts and attributed counts side by side. Counts summed over frames are repeated observations. Never exempt a coordinate solely because it also flashed at +64.
- Use normal maps and HDR for motion, and the existing matched-normal path-tracing workflow for energy. For each run retain executable, shader, input and camera hashes, actual extent, cache capacity, backend, async state and sample phase. Use standalone ZenApp for image and FPS runs and the existing profiler for native 4K GPU traces. [Profiling tool](../../tools/profile_hybrid_gi.py) · [Benchmark launch and input records](../../build/gi-production-check-20261125-1755/assessment.md)

### 1. Reduce the long tail of the lamp-lit bounce estimator

**First experiment.** For the offending probe and nearby good probes, capture a small diagnostic buffer on the landing frame and through +16. Record anchor age and relocation, ray lane and batch, generating strategy, selected lamp/lobe, direction, full mixture PDF, hit position/material, cache hit or direct-light fallback, and the linear contribution before filtering. Rank contributions by size and show which ones reach the flickering pixels. Keep this local to the selected probes and disable diagnostics for timing. A lamp-guided strategy can contribute a large sample even when another proposal has the better density at that direction. [Sampling and hit shading](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/screen_probe_trace.glsl) · [Lamp guide](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/probe_light_sampling.glsl)

**Candidate A — improve sampling coverage at the same budget.** The current shader stratifies lamp selection by lane, selects one of two Henyey–Greenstein lobes using the second coordinate, and stratifies both coordinates across three batches during the first four history frames. With one lamp and all three batches, each local lane already visits both halves of the lobe selector. A forced 50/50 split alone is an inadequate experiment. Measure coverage *per selected lamp* and azimuth, including the first frames after the three-batch stratification ends. When the footprint guide is missing, the two lobes can coincide at `g = .94`; splitting identical lobes provides no benefit. [Trace sequence](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/screen_probe_trace.glsl) · [Batch and slot definitions](../../ZenCore/Include/Graphics/Shared/ScreenProbeGI.h)

Prototype randomized stratification of the joint **lamp × lobe** choices over the available local samples, with an independently permuted angular coordinate. Maintain each lamp’s selection probability, including for small probabilities and odd counts. Preserve the HDR, hemisphere and learned directions and the existing ray counts. For unchanged mixture masses, retain the current full-mixture density. If sample allocation changes the actual mixture, evaluate the corresponding density in both the generator and every estimator that consumes the stored PDF. Keep null below-horizon samples in the normalization. Exercise one lamp, several unequal lamps, coincident lobes, no lamp, and a grazing receiver. Compare sampling histograms and mean projected irradiance against numerical integration of constant illumination and a narrow bright patch.

**Candidate B — improve where the rays point, if A leaves the same tail.** The cached lamp footprint stores the distance and direction of a nearby *visible sample* from the lamp. At a corner this is an imperfect proxy for the lit surface seen by the new probe. Overlay guided directions, actual first-hit points, and high-energy hits from an independent reference batch. Only if those distributions disagree, prototype a second direction or adjusted width learned from the preceding block’s verified hits on the same surface. Keep broad support, use a normalized PDF, invalidate stale guides on light or geometry changes, and separately exercise an empty cache on a first visit. Test A and B independently before combining. [Footprint producer](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/world_cache_shade.glsl) · [Guide consumer](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/probe_light_sampling.glsl)

**Decision.** Advance a candidate when the ledge’s upper-tail error and streak duration improve beyond baseline replay spread, also on the held-out lamp arrangement. Check the whole frame for displaced flashes, the dark-pixel tail, and conservation in the HDR corner fixture. If the diagnostic attributes the dominant weight to hit radiance or visibility instead of directional coverage, follow that producer before tuning either proposal. Preserve the current glossy prefilter while isolating diffuse. [HDR regression](../../tools/check_world_cache_hdr_bounce.py) · [Separate source-additivity regression](../../tools/check_screen_probe_sources.py)

### 2. Give thin, uncovered receivers stable diffuse support

Emit a diagnostic from the gather for **valid receiver with zero compatible weight**, independent of luminance. Record surface identity, geometric normal, depth, base/adaptive winner and any separate environment-source contribution. Overlay the mask on the before-turn, moving and correctly lit reference at identical camera poses. This distinguishes a black wall, an uncovered ornament and a true sky impulse. [Probe placement](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/screen_probe_place.comp) · [Full-resolution gather](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/screen_probe_gather.glsl)

Start with the existing adaptive slot allocation. In the four-tile mode the workgroup currently tests one position in each 2×2 footprint; a one-pixel seam can fall between all candidates. Test edge-aware candidate selection within that footprint on tiles containing multiple receiver identities or depth discontinuities. Retain deterministic tie-breaking, same-surface validation and a bias toward retaining a still-valid anchor to prevent frame-to-frame slot swapping. Measure both holes filled and coverage lost on the surface displaced by the winning probe.

If several surfaces compete for a single slot, test a compact queue of persistently unsupported receiver groups. Cap the work, record overflow, and try a validated same-surface estimate or targeted receiver evaluation only there. Diffuse sky and bounced energy require separate accounting, including already-added environment sources. A world-cache record mixing lighting terms needs an explicit decomposition before it can supply either output. Compare each variant on a thin bright strip next to a dark wall, coplanar material boundaries, a moving occluder, and an unlit closed corner. Reject leaks, double-counted sky, new black pixels and overflow-induced flicker. The exit test is stable, correctly lit named features as well as a lower scene-wide uncovered fraction.

### 3. Identify the lantern’s first incorrect rendering pass

At the same three pixels capture primitive/instance ID and sample coverage, depth, geometric and shading normals, material/opacity, direct diffuse, direct specular, emissive, shadow visibility, translucency and final color on the bad frame and a matched settled frame. Use a supersampled crop as a coverage reference. Find the first pass that differs. Stable coverage plus a spiking glossy term leads to a material specular-AA experiment. Flipping IDs or opacity leads to testing depth ties, alpha coverage or sample placement. A change appearing only in translucency/composition belongs to that pass. Validate on the lantern and a second thin asset. Keep both in the whole-image gate even while the cause is outside GI.

### 4. Escalate only for a residual that the first three stages leave visible

If the lamp estimator is energy-correct but cold probes still lack enough observations, trial **one compatible neighbor’s recorded hit** for a marked young probe. Start in a diagnostic replay with explicit visibility and the correct change of density between the two probe positions. Use a common surface-area measure for a finite hit, account for distance and hit-normal cosine at each origin, and evaluate the mixture of both proposals. Treat sky directions separately. Reject occluded or stale connections. The current six-word hit slot reuses storage for resolved radiance, so first establish which geometry data survives at the reuse point. Measure any additional record bytes, bandwidth, ray queries and pass time before proposing a persistent layout. Compare wall leakage, duplicated outliers and error at equal frame time. [2026 spatial MIS paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2026.3669877) · [Hit record layout](../../Data/Shaders/VoxelGI/screen_probe_hits.glsl) · [Pass owner](../../ZenCore/Source/Graphics/RenderCore/V2/ScreenProbeGI.cpp)

If the raw signal passes and flicker first appears during reconstruction, test a short, surface-validated diffuse reconstruction using coverage, age and signal variance only on the marked region. Test direction reversals, sudden lamp-off events and translated occluders for trails and lag. Reserve layered temporal reuse for a demonstrable *revisit* problem. Retire an experiment when its target signal or cost fails its gate.

## Acceptance and delivery

These are proposed engineering gates. Freeze thresholds and reference poses on the baseline before tuning. Use interleaved baseline/candidate runs; ambiguous differences require more independent phases or runs.

| Gate | Proposed pass condition |
|---|---|
| Visible flashes | Across five independent phases and the held-out trajectories, zero attributable bright streaks or repeatable flashes in the ledge, arch, seams and lantern at native playback speed and frame stepping. At every scored pose/recovery offset the total count stays within its separately measured settled envelope. Start with 0–2 at the reverse pose and recalibrate from independent held-out frames. Track maximum cluster size and lifetime so two large streaks cannot pass as two single pixels. |
| Broader temporal noise | Compare independent sampling phases at the *same* moving frame, then make the identical pairwise measurement on held-out settled frames at that pose. Proposed limit for moving RGB RMS is the settled 95th percentile plus 0.10 levels on the 0–255 scale, globally and in each named crop. The five historical leave-one-out RMS values use a different statistic; measure the paired floor first. Also retain the ordinary-pan baseline as a regression check. Inspect signed moving-minus-reference images and native-speed loops for coherent pulsing, dark flashes, blur or trails that repeat across seeds. A lower bright-cluster count alone cannot pass this gate. |
| Lighting accuracy | On center, 20° and 180° path-traced views, proposed maximum regressions are 0.5 percentage points in absolute mean total/diffuse/specular bias and 1 point in relative RGB RMS per term, accounting for reference uncertainty. Raise reference samples or use paired differences for borderline results. At named thin features require the *correct* reference brightness. Keep the existing HDR linearity and source-additivity tests passing. |
| Frame time | Three interleaved standalone warmed pan runs per build plus three matched 80-frame native 4K Nsight traces, clocks unaltered; separately trace a six-frame turn and its recovery. Reject a reproducible median or p95 slowdown. Use 0.20 ms as the initial non-inferiority margin on the paired p95 difference and repeat when the uncertainty crosses it. Log rays, cache capacity, sparse work/overflow, memory and the 3.60 ms baseline gather. Track the independent 16.67 ms shipping target explicitly. |
| Scope and handoff | Test HDR on/off, normal maps on for motion, light edits, a moving occluder, a cold first visit and a warm revisit. Run native hardware and compute validation for shared shader changes. Preserve a surface-ID mask, term captures, paired timings, hashes and a short playback clip per accepted change. Recheck image quality at 1440p before claiming that tier. |

The first implementation commit should deliver the reproducible motion/coverage test and a per-ray explanation of one failing ledge frame. Follow with independently reviewable lamp sampling, receiver coverage and lantern changes, each carrying its own before/after data. A production acceptance claim follows the combined run on representative game content and target hardware.
