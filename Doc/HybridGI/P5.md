# P5 ray-hit diffuse bounce

2026-10-10. Implements the [P5 contract](../HardwareRayQueryEnvironmentLightingPlan.md#p5-diffuse-bounce-from-ray-hits) and the [P5 review decisions](../HardwareRayQueryEnvironmentLightingPlan.md#p5-review-decisions-2026-10-10). **The P5 exit is met on the RTX 5080:** the RT tier passes the one-bounce, thin-wall, light-change and continuous-motion limits, and both bounce sources are measured on the compute tier.

**Defaults.** `voxel_gi_bounce_source=auto|cone|rays` and the editor's Bounce source control select the path. The default `auto` uses ray-hit bounce wherever the hardware provider is active, as the plan prescribes for the RT tier, and cone bounce on the compute tier, where P8 chooses between them. A smoke run on Sponza (`build/p5/default-auto`) resolves to rays with hardware queries and to cones with `--disable-rt`, with no validation errors. No P6 reflections or P8 preset or budget work is included.

## Implementation

- **Estimator.** `hybrid_trace.glsl` reuses the residual diffuse rays, weighted by their actual mixture PDF, for one bounce. Hardware uses closest hits; the voxel DDA uses its first occupied cell and entered face. Dedicated environment-source rays do not add bounce. With environment lighting disabled, the four residual rays use the cosine proposal.
- **Hit radiance.** `hit_radiance.glsl` reads base-level radiance, normalizes coverage, and requires the cached owner normal to face the incoming ray. It tries one adjacent cell on the ray side of the entered face, then returns zero and counts a rejected lookup. No radiance mip or second bounce is sampled. A voxel ray that starts in an occupied cell blocks, but has no entered face and reads no radiance. An invented face there let the lit side of a wall leak through its base on the compute tier (1.02% before the fix, 0.0010% after).
- **Reconstruction.** Bounce has its own RGBA32F history and RG32F luminance moments, with the existing identity, plane and normal reprojection checks. Its history limit is 16; sky stays at 32. For eight frames after a radiance-cache, bounce-source or intensity change, the limit is 8 and the history is clamped to a ten-sigma box of the current 9×9 neighborhood. Analytic and emissive changes keep sky history.
- **Cache changes keep bounce history** (review decision 1, [responsive policy](../HardwareRayQueryEnvironmentLightingPlan.md#temporal-accumulation)):
  - Before each injection, the base radiance level is copied (ray bounce only).
  - On that frame the bounce rays read both caches at the same hits.
  - Over the same-surface 9×9 neighborhood, the ratio of the new to the previous results, with Beale's bias correction, scales the history per channel.
  - The relative luminance change shortens the history, down to a restart when a light goes out or comes on.
  - A new bounce source or intensity, or more than one injection between frames, resets bounce history.
- **Composition.** Deferred and covered opaque or mask forward composition add exactly one selected bounce term. Unsupported forward layers keep their per-surface path. `H` stays zero, and the seven lighting components remain compatible. Hybrid capture channels 2 and 3 hold raw and reconstructed selected bounce. For rays, raw alpha counts rejected lookups and reconstructed alpha is history length. Metadata records the bounce source and the radiance generation.
- **Generations.** An injection generation is published only after successful execution. The scheduled generation is visible while that same graph is built, so bounce sees the change on the injection frame.

### Memory

| Resource | Cone bounce | Ray bounce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Persistent hybrid histories (two sets) | 160 B/px | 208 B/px (+48) |
| Previous base radiance level | 1 texel | resolution³ × 8 B: 16 MiB at 128³, 128 MiB at 256³ |
| Declared transient formats before graph reuse | 184 B/px | 240 B/px |

Cone bounce allocates 1×1 bounce placeholders, and the passes skip them. Both modes keep the filter's variance target at RGBA32F (+8 B/px per iteration over P4). These are format counts, not measured residency or P8 qualification.

## Review changes (2026-10-10)

1. **Moving lights.** The first implementation discarded bounce history at every radiance-cache change. While a light moved every frame, as the demo's orbiting light does, bounce was one frame of four rays. One frame after such a reset, the RT fixtures measured RMS 16.7% / P99 62% (room) and RMS 132% / P99 448% (thin wall). The light-change limit did not catch it, because it covers one change followed by static frames.
   - **New limit.** A continuous-motion limit was written into the plan before any candidate was measured.
   - **First attempt, rejected.** A test of the old history against new samples alone cannot separate a moderate change from four-ray noise. On the thin wall it kept stale history: frame-8 move bias −9.4%, P99 78%.
   - **Adopted fix.** Correlated lookups of both caches (above) pass every gate. Per-pixel differences were also tried; they inject sparse spikes that the filter does not expect (emissive-off bias +25%). The plain pooled ratio left a −1.1% first-order bias on the sparse thin wall, which Beale's correction reduced to −0.4%.
2. **Sponza metric** (review decision 2). Frozen cameras gate composed diffuse `D_sky + D_bounce`; bounce-only errors are reported. The P5 contract asks for a noise comparison with cone on frozen cameras. In the top view bounce is 0.5% of diffuse irradiance, so the earlier "acceptance blocker" (bounce-channel RMS 27%) judged a signal the image barely contains.
3. **Cone bounce no longer pays for ray bounce.** Bounce histories and transients are 1×1 placeholders unless rays are selected. This removes 48 B/px of persistent memory and the bounce filter work from the cone path, which the compute tier uses by default. Matched cone timings fell by about 0.2–0.3 ms.
4. **One lean gate.** `tools/validate_hybrid_bounce.py` replaces the earlier matrix of 52 coverage runs, the 320×180 duplicate and the repeated suites; see [Verification](#verification-final-build).

## Ground truth

The gate's one-bounce limit is checked against `tools/ground_truth_bounce.py`, the plan's rung-3 one-bounce validation, on CUDA. The gate requires the CUDA backend; a missing backend fails the gate instead of falling back to the CPU.
- **Method.** Mitsuba traces both bounces against the triangles, with point-light shadow rays, and reads no engine voxels or radiance. It first passes its analytic fixtures, then compares every matched pixel of each tier's reference capture with uniform 0.5 albedo, at 4096 samples per pixel. Two independent halves estimate the reference's own noise.
- **Speed.** Both RT fixtures take 16 seconds on the RTX 5080.
- **Replaces.** The earlier Embree CPU spot check, which sampled 2040 and 1256 receivers.

| Fixture | RT-tier bias | RT-tier RMS beyond reference noise | Compute-tier bias | Compute-tier RMS beyond reference noise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Room | −4.52% | 6.2% | −3.96% | 11.4% |
| Thin wall | −4.77% | 9.9% | −3.05% | 16.1% |

Every matched pixel is used (none unmatched). Both tiers lie within the 10% limit. The difference comes from the cache's isotropic cells and resolution. On the compute tier it also comes from hit points at voxel-face entries rather than triangles.

## Limitations

- Each voxel holds one isotropic value for its owner surface, oriented by that surface's authored normal. A two-sided surface lit from behind injects nothing, and rays reaching it from that side are rejected. The room fixture was rewound inward, and the thin wall turned toward its light, so that the fixtures test the contract and not this limitation. In Sponza, 4–9% of bounce rays are rejected lookups.
- Bounce from geometry outside the voxel volume is zero, because hits there have no cache cell.
- The thin-wall move is the tightest light-change margin: P99 19.39% on the RT tier and 19.83% on the compute tier, against 20%.
- Unverified: the remaining P0 environment, resolution and platform matrix; 1080p; bounce settling under camera motion and animated geometry; RX 7900 XT and MoltenVK. P8 qualifies cost and memory.

## Reproduction

Use Python with `tools/requirements-gi-quality.txt`; the `build/dynamic-voxel-m7-quality-20260925/venv` environment was used here.

```powershell
$env:_CL_='/Z7'
build/voxel-gi-build.cmd SpvShaders scene_renderer_demo RenderCoreTest ConeVoxelGIIntegrationTest RayQueryIntegrationTest CommonTest ZenEditorUI
python tools/validate_hybrid_bounce.py --exe build/x64-windows-msvc-debug/bin/scene_renderer_demo.exe --output build/p5/final-hardware --provider hardware --capture --sponza <glTF-Sample-Assets>/Models/Sponza/glTF/Sponza.gltf
python tools/validate_hybrid_bounce.py --exe build/x64-windows-msvc-debug/bin/scene_renderer_demo.exe --output build/p5/final-voxel --provider voxel --capture --sponza <glTF-Sample-Assets>/Models/Sponza/glTF/Sponza.gltf
```

Each gate run captures the fixtures (cone, rays with every light-change stage, and reference), one forward fixture with ray bounce, and Sponza's two frozen views (cone, rays, reference). It then renders the CUDA references once all captures have finished, so the two never share the GPU. It returns nonzero for failed gates; on the compute tier only the fixtures gate. Without `--capture` it re-analyzes saved captures. While iterating, re-run only the capture that a change affects, then the gate once. The demo option `--capture-bounce-lights=PREFIX` captures the light-change stages. `compare_hybrid_gi.py --channel sky|bounce|diffuse` compares standalone captures.
