# P5 ray-hit diffuse bounce

2026-10-22 follow-up: New Sponza's one-bounce light from its 24 imported lights was 33% too dark against the CUDA ground truth at 128³ (floor 56%). The isotropic per-cell cache was the cause; the transport and filtering were correct. Ray hits now take scene lights from a [light-visibility cache](#light-visibility-cache-2026-10-22) and evaluate attenuation and cosine at the hit itself. New Sponza is within 5% at 64³, 128³ and 256³, and the fixture, bounce and native gates pass on both tiers.

2026-10-11 follow-up: steady diffuse bounce now uses the configured 32-frame history instead of a 16-frame cap. Hotel hall's composed shipping gate now passes; the independent bounce, thin-wall and lighting-response gates pass again. See the [current acceptance record](P0P4Acceptance.md). The original measurements and their 16-frame reconstruction below remain historical evidence.

2026-10-10. Implements the [P5 contract](../HardwareRayQueryEnvironmentLightingPlan.md#p5-diffuse-bounce-from-ray-hits) and the [P5 review decisions](../HardwareRayQueryEnvironmentLightingPlan.md#p5-review-decisions-2026-10-10). **The P5 exit is met on the RTX 5080:** the RT tier passes the one-bounce, thin-wall, light-change and continuous-motion limits, and both bounce sources are measured on the compute tier.

**Defaults.** `voxel_gi_bounce_source=auto|cone|rays` and the editor's Bounce source control select the path. The default `auto` uses ray-hit bounce wherever the hardware provider is active, as the plan prescribes for the RT tier, and cone bounce on the compute tier, where P8 chooses between them. A smoke run on Sponza (`build/p5/default-auto`) resolves to rays with hardware queries and to cones with `--disable-rt`, with no validation errors. No P6 reflections or P8 preset or budget work is included.

## Implementation

- **Estimator.** `hybrid_trace.glsl` reuses the residual diffuse rays, weighted by their actual mixture PDF, for one bounce. Hardware uses closest hits; the voxel DDA uses its first occupied cell and entered face. Dedicated environment-source rays do not add bounce. With environment lighting disabled, the four residual rays use the cosine proposal.
- **Hit radiance.** `hit_radiance.glsl` reads base-level radiance, normalizes coverage, and requires the cached owner normal to face the incoming ray. It tries one adjacent cell on the ray side of the entered face, then returns zero and counts a rejected lookup. No radiance mip or second bounce is sampled. Since 2026-10-22, with ray bounce and triangle visibility, this isotropic value holds emission, sky light and uncached lights only; scene lights come from the [light-visibility cache](#light-visibility-cache-2026-10-22), and a lookup counts as rejected only when neither part is found. A voxel ray that starts in an occupied cell blocks, but has no entered face and reads no radiance. An invented face there let the lit side of a wall leak through its base on the compute tier (1.02% before the fix, 0.0010% after).
- **Reconstruction.** Bounce has its own RGBA32F history and RG32F luminance moments, with the existing identity, plane and normal reprojection checks. Its history limit is 16; sky stays at 32. For eight frames after a radiance-cache, bounce-source or intensity change, the limit is 8 and the history is clamped to a ten-sigma box of the current 9×9 neighborhood. Analytic and emissive changes keep sky history.
- **Cache changes keep bounce history** (review decision 1, [responsive policy](../HardwareRayQueryEnvironmentLightingPlan.md#temporal-accumulation)):
  - Before each injection, the base radiance level is copied (ray bounce only).
  - On that frame the bounce rays read both caches at the same hits.
  - Over the same-surface 9×9 neighborhood, the ratio of the new to the previous results, with Beale's bias correction, scales the history per channel.
  - The relative luminance change shortens the history, down to a restart when a light goes out or comes on.
  - A new bounce source or intensity, or more than one injection between frames, resets bounce history.
- **Composition.** Deferred and covered opaque or mask forward composition add exactly one selected bounce term. Unsupported forward layers keep their per-surface path. `H` stays zero, and the seven lighting components remain compatible. Hybrid capture channels 2 and 3 hold raw and reconstructed selected bounce. For rays, raw alpha counts rejected lookups and reconstructed alpha is history length. Metadata records the bounce source and the radiance generation.
- **Generations.** An injection generation is published only after successful execution. The scheduled generation is visible while that same graph is built, so bounce sees the change on the injection frame.

## Review changes (2026-10-10)

1. **Moving lights.** The first implementation discarded bounce history at every radiance-cache change. While a light moved every frame, as the demo's orbiting light does, bounce was one frame of four rays. One frame after such a reset, the RT fixtures measured RMS 16.7% / P99 62% (room) and RMS 132% / P99 448% (thin wall). The light-change limit did not catch it, because it covers one change followed by static frames.
   - **New limit.** A continuous-motion limit was written into the plan before any candidate was measured.
   - **First attempt, rejected.** A test of the old history against new samples alone cannot separate a moderate change from four-ray noise. On the thin wall it kept stale history: frame-8 move bias −9.4%, P99 78%.
   - **Adopted fix.** Correlated lookups of both caches (above) pass every gate. Per-pixel differences were also tried; they inject sparse spikes that the filter does not expect (emissive-off bias +25%). The plain pooled ratio left a −1.1% first-order bias on the sparse thin wall, which Beale's correction reduced to −0.4%.
2. **Sponza metric** (review decision 2). Frozen cameras gate composed diffuse `D_sky + D_bounce`; bounce-only errors are reported. The P5 contract asks for a noise comparison with cone on frozen cameras. In the top view bounce is 0.5% of diffuse irradiance, so the earlier "acceptance blocker" (bounce-channel RMS 27%) judged a signal the image barely contains.
3. **Cone bounce no longer pays for ray bounce.** Bounce histories and transients are 1×1 placeholders unless rays are selected. This removes 48 B/px of persistent memory and the bounce filter work from the cone path, which the compute tier uses by default. Matched cone timings fell by about 0.2–0.3 ms.
4. **One lean gate.** `tools/validate_hybrid_bounce.py` replaces the earlier matrix of 52 coverage runs, the 320×180 duplicate and the repeated suites; see [Verification](#verification-final-build).

## Ground truth

The gate's one-bounce limit is checked against `tools/ground_truth_bounce.py`, the plan's rung-3 one-bounce validation, on CUDA. The gate requires the CUDA backend; a missing backend fails the gate instead of falling back to the CPU.
- **Method.** Mitsuba traces both bounces against the triangles, with point-light shadow rays, and reads no engine voxels or radiance. It first passes its analytic fixtures, then compares every matched pixel of each tier's reference capture with uniform 0.5 albedo, at 4096 samples per pixel. Two independent halves estimate the reference's own noise.
- **Speed.** Both RT fixtures take 16 seconds on the RTX 5080.
- **Replaces.** The earlier Embree CPU spot check, which sampled 2040 and 1256 receivers.

## Light-visibility cache (2026-10-22)

New Sponza (`E:/Dev/main_sponza/NewSponza_Main_glTF_003.gltf`, every material made untextured 0.5 albedo) lights its hall with 24 imported lights at glTF's default intensity: a 1 lux sun and 23 lamps of 1 cd, most hanging in lanterns with opaque glass. Measured with the HW RT tier, triangle-ray bounce, analytic shadows and environment off. Converged captures used 256 × 256 frames at 480 × 270, against `tools/ground_truth_bounce.py` on CUDA (4096 samples). Bounce was 33% too dark at 128³, 41% at 64³ and 28% at 256³, and the floor 49–63%. The shipping output equalled the converged capture, so reconstruction was not the cause.

### Diagnosis

Each row replaces the cache lookup at every bounce hit with an emulation, at 128³ (bias over all receivers / floor):

| Bounce-hit radiance | Bias |
| --- | --- |
| Isotropic cache (before) | −33.3% / −55.8% |
| Exact lighting at the hit: 24 shadow rays per hit | −0.5% / −1.1% |
| Exact, but lookups the cache rejects contribute zero | −9.9% / −9.6% |
| Exact, but the light cosine uses the cell's stored normal | −23.6% / −36.6% |
| Cosine at the hit, visibility and attenuation from the cell owner's point | −30.2% / −55.7% |
| The hit's own triangle as owner, visibility and attenuation from the point nearest the cell center | −4.0% / −11.6% |

- **The owner surface.** The cache stores one isotropic value per cell, for the lowest-numbered triangle in it and its normal. 22% of the visible receivers in this view lie in cells whose owner normal differs from their own by more than 45°: arch soffits owned by the arch fronts, and a checkerboard of owners around round columns. The owner's point is often on the side of a column or arch facing away from a light, where the shadow ray starts inside the solid. 92% of the owner-visibility deficit came from owners on a differently oriented plane. Neighbor search for a matching cell only reached −29%, because whole soffit strips have no cell owned by the soffit.
- **Lamp attenuation.** With the right triangle, the sun was within 1% (−0.7% / −0.8% against a sun-only CUDA truth), but the lamps were −8.9% / −20.4% low. Attenuation evaluated at the hit fixed them (+1.3% / +2.4%); visibility at the hit did not (−10.8% / −25.2%). 91% of the floor's lamp bounce comes from secondary hits within one cell (25 cm) of a lamp, mostly lantern interiors seen through their openings.
- **Rejections and diffuse weight.** Rejected lookups (8% of rays) cost about 9%. The 0.96 dielectric diffuse weight costs 4% against the reference's Lambertian albedo; it also explains most of the fixtures' −4.5%, and is unchanged.

### Design

- **Class owners.** Voxelization keeps, beside the owner, the lowest-numbered triangle of each of six surface classes per cell: the dominant axis of the triangle's normal, turned toward its authored vertex normal, and that normal's sign (`voxel_surface_class.glsl`). Signed classes separate the inside and the outside of a lantern pane and the two faces of thin geometry; three unsigned classes reached only −16% in emulation. The class grid is at most 128³ (`ZEN_LIGHT_CACHE_MAX_RESOLUTION`), so 256³ shares 128³ class cells.
- **Injection.** For every class with an owner, injection traces each of the first 31 scene lights once. The ray starts from the owner point nearest the cell center, moved 0.1 class cells off the surface along the class side of its normal. A one-bit mask per class records which lights reach that class (`light_visibility_cache.glsl`). The offset recovers lights behind a curved owner's tangent plane that other surfaces of its class face. Larger offsets let sunlight pass occluders: the sun-only bias was −6% at 0, +1% at 0.1 and +9% at 0.25 cells. The cache also stores the lights it traced, so a hit evaluates the injected lights, not the current ones.
- **Lookup.** A ray hit finds the class of its own geometric normal, in its class cell or the neighbor on the ray's side. For each visible light it evaluates attenuation and cosine at the hit. Point and spot lights within 1.5 class cells of the cell center are traced from the hit itself; injection sets their bits so the lookup visits set bits only. Without these rays the floor was 23% low at 128³ and 35% at 64³; a 2-cell radius measured the same as 1.5. The lit sum is weighted by the hit cell's diffuse reflectance, as the isotropic cache weights it.
- **Isotropic part.** Emission, sky light, the camera light and scene lights from the 32nd on stay in the isotropic radiance, read with the unchanged owner-normal rule. A thin wall's unlit side still receives nothing: its class has no owner there, or the light lies behind it.
- **History.** The cache follows the radiance chain: copied with the base level before an injection with ray bounce, swapped with the alternate chain under previous-frame radiance, and snapshotted with it. The correlated previous-cache lookups therefore use the previous masks and lights. New cache buffers are initialized from their first injection, so the graph never reads undefined history.
- **Activation.** The cache is active with ray bounce and triangle visibility (`gi.limits.w`). Otherwise injection writes the full isotropic radiance as before and the caches are 2 KiB placeholders. A cache that does not fit a storage buffer falls back to the isotropic path with a warning.

## Limitations

- Since 2026-10-22, scene lights reach ray hits through per-class visibility ([above](#light-visibility-cache-2026-10-22)). Visibility is one sample per class and class cell, so penumbrae are as coarse as the class grid, and an occluder thinner than the 0.1-cell offset can be missed. Rays that hit the unlit face of a single-sided surface find no class there and receive no scene light. The camera light, scene lights from the 32nd on, sky light and emission keep the isotropic owner rule below.
- Each voxel holds one isotropic value for its owner surface, oriented by that surface's authored normal. A two-sided surface lit from behind injects nothing, and rays reaching it from that side are rejected. The room fixture was rewound inward, and the thin wall turned toward its light, so that the fixtures test the contract and not this limitation. In Sponza, 4–9% of bounce rays are rejected lookups.
- Bounce from geometry outside the voxel volume is zero, because hits there have no cache cell.
- The thin-wall move is the tightest light-change margin: P99 19.39% on the RT tier and 19.83% on the compute tier, against 20%.
- Unverified: the remaining P0 environment, resolution and platform matrix; 1080p; bounce settling under camera motion and animated geometry; RX 7900 XT and MoltenVK. P8 qualifies cost and memory.
